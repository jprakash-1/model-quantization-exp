Step 1 of 7

Start here

Quantization stores a model's weights with fewer bits. That shrinks memory and speeds up generation, but every rounding step moves the model a little away from the original. This guide shows how far, on real models and real engines.

Three ideas to carry through every tab
  1. Bits are a budget. Fewer bits per weight means less VRAM and faster decoding, because generating each token reads every weight once.
  2. Rounding error is uneven. Most weights survive; a few sensitive ones do not. Good methods spend their bits where the error hurts.
  3. The benchmark decides what you see. Multiple-choice scores barely move at 4 bits; free-form generation and strict output formats show the damage first.
Try it: round some weights

What “4-bit” actually does to a weight

48 synthetic weights, rounded to the nearest of 2bits evenly spaced levels between their min and max. This is plain round-to-nearest, the simplest method on this page.

Qwen3.5-9B

Source: computingforgeeks · vLLM 0.30.0, llm-compressor 0.14.0, llama.cpp (29 Sep 2026) · NVIDIA A10G 24GB

All checkpoints were quantized from the same BF16 weights on one box. GSM8K is lm-eval-harness, 5-shot, greedy, all 1,319 questions. KL divergence is per-token against BF16 logits on wikitext-2 (150 × 512 tokens). vLLM and llama.cpp speeds come from different benchmark tools, so compare speed only within each table.

What to notice
  • FP8 matched BF16 exactly on GSM8K (87.5%) and was 1.76× faster.
  • Both 4-bit vLLM formats lost 6 to 12 GSM8K points; GGUF IQ4_XS lost about 1.
  • Perplexity barely moves even where KL divergence and top-1 agreement show real drift.

LLaMA3-8B

Source: Huang et al., arXiv 2404.14047 · Macaronlin/LLaMA3-Quantization

Research PTQ methods across 2 to 8 bits. Calibration is 128 WikiText2 samples at 2,048 tokens; grouped methods use group size 128. Accuracy is the zero-shot average of PIQA, ARC-e, ARC-c, HellaSwag and Winogrande. This study does not report inference VRAM.

What to notice
  • Calibrated 4-bit methods keep about 98–99% of the FP16 average.
  • Plain rounding (RTN) collapses at 3 bits; GPTQ, AWQ, SliM-LLM and QuIP hold up.
  • At 2 bits almost everything falls to chance.

Gemma 3 27B (Unsloth Dynamic GGUF)

Source: Unsloth docs, Dynamic GGUFs · vendor-published results for its own quants

Unsloth's Dynamic quants pick a per-layer bit mix and use a larger imatrix calibration set. They quantized both the BF16 checkpoint and Google's QAT checkpoint across the full llama.cpp ladder and scored 5-shot MMLU. BF16 MMLU is 71.5. Their newer Dynamic 3.0 results for Qwen3.8-27B are published only as images, so they are not tabulated here.

What to notice
  • MMLU is flat down to 3-bit and still 96% at Q2_K_XL.
  • Below 2 bits, accuracy falls fast.
  • Unsloth's Dynamic quants cut KL divergence 3–9% for 1–3% more disk.

Findings

What holds when the same pattern shows up in more than one source. Then test yourself.

Nine patterns

Patterns that hold across more than one source. Each line of evidence links to where the number comes from.

Your GPU decides the format before accuracy does

FP8 with FP8 matmuls needs Ada or Hopper in vLLM; on Ampere it runs weight-only. TensorRT-LLM's FP8 per-tensor recipe skips Ampere entirely, and NVFP4 runs natively only on Blackwell. INT4 W4A16 is the one format every major engine runs from Ampere upward.

vLLM hardware table · TensorRT-LLM matrix · SGLang docs

Batching engine matters more than kernel speed under load

On an RTX 5090, single-stream decode was within about 11% across vLLM, llama.cpp and Ollama (76.8–85.7 tok/s). At 32 concurrent requests, vLLM delivered 936 tok/s, llama.cpp 369 and Ollama 74. Among datacenter engines on the same FP8 70B model, the spread at 100 concurrent was only 2,400 to 2,780 tok/s.

computingforgeeks engines · Spheron

Naive 4-bit breaks small VLMs

Quantizing every layer of Qwen2-VL-2B with bitsandbytes 4-bit produced a wrong answer; keeping a handful of sensitive layers in 16-bit fixed it for 0.45 GB. SGLang reports near-zero accuracy for one GPTQ variant of Qwen2.5-VL-7B. Vision models need per-layer care.

Unsloth dynamic 4-bit · SGLang docs

8-bit is free

FP8 dynamic, INT8 weight-only and W8A8 SmoothQuant land within noise of full precision in every study that tested them. On a 24GB card, FP8 on Qwen3.5-9B also cut latency from 4.68 s to 2.66 s and raised KV capacity from about 18K to 166K tokens.

Qwen FP8 GSM8K 87.5 = BF16 87.5 · LLaMA3-8B AWQ-W8 68.7 vs 68.6 · Llama 3.1 405B W8A8-FP 86.89 vs 86.79 on Open LLM v1 · cfg · 2404.14047 · 2411.02355

4-bit loss depends on the task, not the method label

Calibrated 4-bit methods keep about 98–99% on multiple-choice and DocVQA. On GSM8K, a generative task with a strict answer format, uniform W4A16 on Qwen3.5-9B lost 6 to 12 points. Likelihood-scored benchmarks understate damage to free-form generation.

LLaMA3-8B AWQ-4 68.2/68.6 · LLaVA DocVQA AWQ-4 77.2/78.2 · Qwen GPTQ 81.8/87.5, AWQ strict 75.7/87.5 · Llama 3.1 8B W4A16 Arena-Hard 24.0 vs 25.8 · 2411.02355

Output-format drift is a separate failure mode

AWQ on Qwen3.5-9B solved about as many problems as GPTQ (82.0 flexible) but broke the required answer format more often (75.7 strict). Any pipeline that parses structured output should be scored on its exact schema.

Strict − flexible gap: AWQ −6.3 pts, GPTQ +1.0, FP8 +0.5 (derived) · cfg, AWQ vs GPTQ

Bits per weight do not predict VRAM

The 4-bit vLLM checkpoints put 7.3 GiB on the GPU, while GGUF Q4_K_M uses 5.4 GiB, because llm-compressor kept the 248K-vocab embedding and output head in BF16. On Llama-2-13B, AWQ used 0.9 to 1.9 GB more VRAM than GPTQ at the same group size.

cfg, why AWQ is bigger than GGUF · oobabooga, AWQ

Perplexity hides most of the damage

Q4_K_M perplexity sits within 0.3% of BF16, yet it picks a different next token 7.5% of the time. Q5_K_M even scores lower perplexity than BF16. KL divergence and a task metric catch what perplexity misses.

Q4_K_M: PPL 7.755 vs 7.738 · top-1 agree 92.5% · mean KLD 0.034 · cfg, myths

3 bits is a cliff for naive rounding; 2 bits needs size and mixed precision

RTN drops from 63.9 at 4 bits to 40.2 at 3 bits on LLaMA3-8B. Calibrated methods hold 61.7 to 64.5 at 3 bits. At 2 bits, uniform PTQ on an 8B model falls to near chance on text and to no output on vision-language tasks. A 27B model with a mixed-precision GGUF mix (Unsloth Q2_K_XL) still keeps 96% of MMLU.

LLaMA3-8B GPTQ-2 36.2/68.6 · Gemma 3 27B Q2_K_XL 68.7/71.5 MMLU · 2404.14047 · Unsloth
For clinical extraction and note generation: serve FP8 on vLLM or SGLang if the GPUs are Ada or Hopper; on Ampere, FP8 still halves weight memory but runs weight-only. Move to INT4 W4A16 only if memory or concurrency forces it. For Qwen2.5-VL-style extraction, keep the vision encoder unquantized and validate the exact checkpoint in the exact engine. Score every candidate on entity-level recall for rare clinical terms, strict JSON schema validity, and KL divergence on your own transcripts. These are recommendations drawn from the evidence above, not results from a source.

Where every number comes from and how it was checked.

How this was verified

What is quoted, what is computed, and what the sources themselves get wrong.

  1. Collection. Every source page was fetched on 16 Oct 2026 and its tables transcribed. Numbers published only as chart images (LMSYS, BentoML, most of Unsloth Dynamic 3.0) are described in words and not tabulated.
  2. Derived values. Retention and recovery percentages, the Pareto frontier, strict-minus-flexible gaps, KLD and size changes, and “extra over bnb 4-bit” are computed on this page from the quoted numbers. Each table's citation line says when a column is derived.
  3. Automated check. A script compared all 728 numeric values in this page's data tables against the tables copied from each fetched source page: 0 mismatches. The few hand-entered tables (Hugging Face blog, Unsloth headline claims, the at-a-glance cards) were checked by eye; the Hugging Face blog latencies and memory are rounded from the published values.
  4. Inconsistencies inside sources. arXiv 2411.02355 prints several recovery percentages with a stray leading digit (for example 199.31 for 99.31), so recovery is recomputed from raw scores. The engines article gives the RTX 5090 vLLM single stream as 82 tok/s in its text and 85.7 in its table; the table is used. Unsloth's dynamic 4-bit post claims under 10% extra VRAM, while its own table shows 33% for Qwen2-VL-2B.
  5. Comparability. Each study uses its own hardware, runtime versions, prompts and metrics. Compare within a table. Perplexity values are never comparable across model families because tokenizers differ.
  6. Vendor sources. Unsloth (its own quants and fine-tuning library) and Spheron (a GPU cloud) publish results about products they sell or host. Their numbers are shown as published and labelled as such.
